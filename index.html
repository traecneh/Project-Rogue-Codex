1999
Official lineage

Dransik’s Early Worlds

The original Dransik takes shape, years before the Classic name.Read the storyClose story ⌄

Asylumsoft’s archived news announces the Worlds of Dransik webpage on June 22, 1999, pre-alpha client 0.10 on July 9, and working player-versus-player melee on August 4. These are dated development milestones, rather than a definitive first launch date.

A September 29 update describes isometric-engine work while the top-down game remains in use. A November announcement dates Asylumsoft’s opening for business to October 28, 1999.

The original game was called Dransik. It later became known as Dransik Classic, continuing alongside the newer isometric game.

Jun 12, 2002
Official lineage

Dransik Opens Paid Service

YouCanGame opens paid accounts for the original Dransik.Read the storyClose story ⌄

Following a June 6 distribution announcement, Dransik’s June 12 news says YouCanGame is open and directs beta players to activate paid accounts. Subsequent notices describe events and server updates.

YouCanGame’s June FAQ lists subscriptions at $4.99 per month for existing beta players and $6.99 for new players, with a 14-day payment grace period. Free testing continued separately.

This commercial service preceded the newer isometric game’s March 2003 release. An October 2002 guide identifies YouCanGame’s paid server as the old-graphics version.

The Dransik Split

Classic and the newer isometric Dransik coexist as distinct versions.Read the storyClose story ⌄

Jeff Barrett’s October 2002 guide distinguishes an older-graphics YouCanGame server from the new isometric Dransik New World beta. Their quest and guild systems also differed.

The guide lists New World, Krythan Inception in California, Great Wall in Asia, and Mystery and Magic, an experimental magic-system server. These were server variants of the newer game—not four separate descendants.

The newer branch is also referred to in historical accounts as New World / Primordia / ISO and later becomes Ashen Empires.

Apr 2004
Inspired descendant

Vorlia’s Early Alpha

Covad’s hobby project develops its own Dransik-inspired world.Read the storyClose story ⌄

A September 2004 archive preserves Vorlia releases dated April 7 and April 11. Alpha 0.0036 includes mining and guild functionality; 0.0037 adds PvP, guards, safe zones, smelting, and blacksmithing. The site describes a free hobby project seeking alpha testers.

Later releases add areas and equipment, Dransik-style line of sight in October, and elemental damage and resistances in November. In January 2005, rheo joins to help with coding.

The original credits identify Covad as main coder, MDF as secondary coder, rheo as editor coder, DevoC for design, and Spide for graphics. Jason Ely / Lothgar is thanked for Dransik’s inspiration.

Arakard’s clarification, shared with the Codex maintainer, states that the original Vorlia/Project Rogue code does not overlap with Dransik’s source and that their networking was substantially different.

The April releases are documented milestones, not a confirmed first-ever launch. MDF’s expanded name remains unverified.

Shel revives a Dransik-inspired hobby MMORPG.Read the storyClose story ⌄

The existing Codex dates the Project Rogue / Rogue Online branch to July 2017 and credits Arakard and Acry with rebooting Vorlia. Its account describes revised stats, Dransik-era systems, and PvP changes.

According to Arakard’s Discord clarification, the original Vorlia/Project Rogue codebase does not overlap with Dransik’s source code. The connection to Dransik is through inspiration and game systems, rather than shared source code.

A community account relayed by the Codex maintainer identifies Zonorian as Arakard’s main inspiration. Zonorian’s own site documents its Dransik influence; the specific features that influenced Project Rogue remain unrecorded here. This is a reported design influence, not evidence of shared code.

The Vorlia source release is a preceding milestone, not a proven delivery route. The exact source version and transfer history remain open research questions.

Archive account: the founder/date account comes from the existing Codex. Direct provenance for the source transfer has not been established here.
